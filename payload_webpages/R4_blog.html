Why Remote Work Is Reshaping Australian Cities

By Rebecca Nolan, Contributing Columnist

Five years on from the pandemic-driven shift to remote work, Australian cities are still adjusting to a fundamentally different relationship between employees and office space. What began as a temporary necessity has, for many industries, become a permanent feature of working life.

Commercial real estate data shows CBD office vacancy rates remain elevated across Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane compared to pre-2020 levels. Property analysts suggest this reflects a lasting shift rather than a temporary dip, with many companies embracing hybrid arrangements as a long-term strategy rather than a stopgap measure.

"We're not seeing a return to five-day office weeks in most sectors. The data consistently points to hybrid becoming the default, not the exception," said one workplace strategist interviewed for this piece.

The shift has had flow-on effects for surrounding businesses. Cafes and retailers that once relied on weekday commuter traffic have had to adapt, with many pivoting toward weekend trade or diversifying their offerings entirely.

Not everyone agrees the shift is entirely positive. Some economists have raised concerns about the long-term impact on mentorship and informal knowledge transfer that traditionally occurred in physical office settings, particularly for early-career employees.

Regardless of where the debate lands, one thing seems clear: the Australian workplace of 2026 looks very different from the one that existed before 2020, and there is little indication of a full reversion on the horizon.
