The Perfect Weeknight Pasta: 20-Minute Garlic Butter Shrimp

By Olivia Marsh, Food Editor
★★★★★ 4.8 from 312 reviews

Some nights call for something quick but still special, and this garlic butter shrimp pasta hits that mark every single time. It comes together in under twenty minutes using mostly pantry staples, and the buttery, garlicky sauce clings to every strand of pasta in the best possible way.

Ingredients

  • 400g linguine or spaghetti
  • 500g large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup dry white wine (or chicken stock)
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1/4 cup chopped fresh parsley
  • Salt and cracked black pepper, to taste
  • Chilli flakes, optional

Method

Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil and cook the pasta according to packet instructions until al dente. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining.

While the pasta cooks, melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook for about 30 seconds until fragrant, being careful not to let it burn. Add the shrimp in a single layer and cook for 1-2 minutes per side until pink and just cooked through, then remove to a plate.

Pour the wine into the same skillet and let it simmer for a minute, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom. Stir in the lemon juice, then return the shrimp to the pan along with the drained pasta. Toss everything together, adding splashes of the reserved pasta water until the sauce coats the noodles nicely.

Finish with the chopped parsley, a generous crack of black pepper, and chilli flakes if you like a bit of heat. Serve immediately while everything is hot and glossy.

Tips for Success

Don't overcook the shrimp — they turn rubbery fast, so pull them from the heat the moment they curl into a "C" shape. If you're short on white wine, chicken stock with a small splash of extra lemon juice works beautifully as a substitute.

This recipe also scales well for meal prep. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to two days, though the pasta will absorb some of the sauce, so a splash of water when reheating helps bring it back to life.
